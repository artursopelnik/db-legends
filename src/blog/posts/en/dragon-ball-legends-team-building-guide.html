In Dragon Ball Legends PvP, the strongest single fighter regularly loses to the better team. Three things decide whether six characters become a real team: aligned Z-Abilities, covered roles and matching equipment. This guide walks through all three.

Six fighters, one plan

A team always has six slots: three fighters battle, three sit on the bench. The bench is not decoration – every character contributes their Z-Ability to the whole team, whether they fight or not. That's why the last three slots are picked for their Z-Ability boosts, not their combat strength.

Z-Abilities: build around one tag

Z-Abilities boost stats for specific tags – for example "Son Family", "Fusion Warrior" or "Regeneration". The rule of thumb: pick one main tag and make sure all six characters boost it and benefit from it. A team where every Z-Ability hits every fighter easily gains double-digit percentages in attack and defense compared to a wild mix of strong individual units.

Cover the three roles

A solid core needs three jobs done: a damage dealer who ends matches, a defensive fighter you can safely switch to under pressure, and a support that heals or buffs. Two damage dealers plus one defender also works – but without any defensive option, one bad exchange loses the match.

Equipment: three slots, one rule

Every fighter has three equipment slots. The simple rule: match the main stat. Strike attackers get strike-attack equips, blast attackers get blast equips, defenders get health and defense. Tag-restricted equipment (e.g. "Fusion Warrior only") rolls higher values than generic pieces – another reason to build around one tag.

Common mistakes

  • Six damage dealers: impressive on paper, folds under pressure.
  • Ignoring the bench: three empty-feeling slots are 30–40% missing stats.
  • Chasing every banner: a finished, well-equipped team beats a collection of shiny new units.
